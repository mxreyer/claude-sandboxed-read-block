## Caveats you need for reading this

**The sandbox could not launch any command.** Every test that got past the permission layer died identically on `bwrap: loopback: Failed RTM_NEWADDR: Operation not permitted`. A bare `pwd` fails the same way, so this is the bubblewrap setup in this environment, not anything about the test commands. For C2, C3, C7 and C8, "ran OK" vs. "the read block would have stopped it at runtime" is therefore **untested** — all I established is that they were *not* denied pre-execution.

**Deviations from your protocol, both disclosed:**
- I first issued C1–C3 as three parallel Bash calls. C2 and C3 failed on `bwrap`, which I initially read as a concurrency artifact, so I re-ran each once serially. Same failure. No denial was ever retried.
- I ran one extra `pwd` as a diagnostic to isolate the `bwrap` failure.

**The post-C4/C7 "return to root" step was never needed** — neither command executed, so no `cd` took effect. I can't positively confirm the cwd, since `pwd` itself can't run.

## The one substantive finding

C4 and C7 split, and that's the interesting result:

- **C4** (`cd <dir> && cat <relative>`) → denied on the `cd`.
- **C7** (`cd <dir> && cat …; cd <dir> && cat …`) → **not denied**, passed through to execution.

Same `cd sandboxed-read-block && cat hooks/hooks.json` prefix in both. Adding a second `; cd … && cat …` segment made the checker stop objecting to the first one. Worth a look at how the multi-operation split path feeds segments to the `cd` check — C6 shows multi-segment commands take a different route (generic "requires approval" rather than the read-block message), and C7 suggests the per-segment `cd` analysis isn't applied on that route.
